Meta's WhatsApp Business Agent Is Live. Read This Before You Turn It On.

The AI receptionist is now built into the platform, and it's free — until it isn't. What Israeli small businesses should check first.

On June 3, at its Conversations conference in London, Meta flipped a switch it had been quietly testing for almost two years: the Meta Business Agent, an AI that answers your customers for you inside WhatsApp and Instagram DMs, is now available to businesses worldwide. More than a million businesses were already using early versions in pilot markets like India and Mexico before the global rollout.

I build WhatsApp bots for Israeli businesses for a living, so you might expect me to tell you this is terrible news and quietly hope you never notice the feature exists. It's the opposite. Meta building an AI receptionist into the platform is the strongest signal yet that this category is real. But after 50-plus bot deployments into Israeli businesses, I can also tell you where the built-in agent will shine, where it will quietly embarrass you, and what to check before you let it talk to your customers.

What Meta actually shipped

The Business Agent answers customer questions, recommends products, books appointments, qualifies sales leads, and hands the conversation to a human when it gets stuck. There is nothing to install and nothing to integrate: it learns from your Facebook page, your past chats, and your website, if you have one.

You also control how much rope it gets. There's a fully manual mode, a "suggestions" mode where the AI drafts replies and you approve them, and a full autopilot mode where the agent handles conversations on its own until they cross a threshold you define — at which point a human takes over.

That last sentence is where most of the risk lives, and we'll come back to it.

The free period is the product demo

Right now the agent costs nothing, and Meta has been clear that this won't last. Pricing is being folded into WhatsApp Business Premium subscription tiers, with token-based billing (you pay per volume of AI-generated text) for larger accounts. Industry trackers report per-token charges already began rolling out on August 1.

If that sounds unfamiliar, remember that Meta already charges Israeli businesses real money on WhatsApp: a marketing template message to an Israeli number costs about 3.5 cents, and a utility message about half a cent, per message. The AI agent is the next monetization layer on top of that rate card. Nothing wrong with it — just don't build your staffing plan around a "free AI employee." It's an introductory price, not a business model.

Where it will genuinely help

If half of your WhatsApp day is copy-pasting the same six answers — opening hours, do you deliver to Ashdod, is this in stock, how do I get to you — the built-in agent will pay for itself even after billing starts. For a one-person Israeli business drowning in repetitive questions, "turn on the built-in AI" is honestly the right first step, and I say that as someone who sells the alternative.

The Israeli fine print: three things to check first

1. Hebrew. Meta's launch materials say the agent learns from your page and your website. They say nothing about Hebrew quality. In our own testing of AI models for Hebrew, in both chat and voice, the gap between the English demo and Hebrew production shows up fast: gendered address that alienates half your customers, slang it takes literally, and confident answers to questions it misread. Before you let it near customers, test it in Hebrew with real customer phrasing — the messy, typo-ridden, no-punctuation kind — not the tidy sentences from your FAQ page.

2. The spam law. Israel's anti-spam law allows statutory damages of up to ₪1,000 per unsolicited marketing message, with no need to prove actual harm. An agent that "qualifies sales leads" is an agent that initiates commercial messaging on your behalf. If it follows up with someone who never gave consent, you are the one liable — not Meta. The law does not care whether a human or a model pressed send. Whatever automation you use, built-in or custom, your consent records need to be in order first.

3. The answers it invents from stale data. Last month, a home seller in the middle of a conversation with one of our real-estate listing bots stopped answering questions and asked one of his own: "Who are you?" Customers probe. They test. And an agent that learned your business from your website will answer with whatever your website says — so if your site still lists 2023 prices, the agent will quote them, confidently, in writing, to a customer who screenshots it. Feed it current information or don't feed it at all.

Escalation is the whole game

The businesses that get burned by this won't be burned because the AI answered "what are your hours" badly. They'll be burned because it answered a price objection, a complaint, or a cancellation request on autopilot. The single most important setting in the entire product is the threshold where a human takes over.

My advice: start in suggestions mode. Watch what the AI wants to say for two weeks. Promote it to autopilot only on the question types where its drafts were consistently right, and keep price, complaints, and anything involving money on the human side of the line.

Where a custom bot still wins

To be fair to my own trade: the built-in agent lives entirely inside Meta's walls. It can't create an invoice in your Israeli billing software, check availability in your actual calendar system, push a lead into your CRM, or run a multi-step flow like deposit-then-booking-then-reminder. As an Israeli WhatsApp bot developer I'm obviously biased, so discount accordingly — but here is the pattern I expect over the next year: Meta's agent becomes the entry point, businesses discover what conversational automation does to their response times, and then they hit the walls and want the version that talks to their own systems.

Bottom line

The AI receptionist just moved from "a thing agencies sell you" to "a thing built into the platform your customers already use." That's good for everyone. Turn it on — in suggestions mode, in Hebrew, with your consent records in order and your website prices current — and read the fine print before your customers write it for you.
